    Money

    Who pays whom

    The power goes into the street's grid like always. The meters and the split table decide whose it is. Then the buildings settle the money between themselves.

    Buildings that give power earn, a year–
    Buildings that get power save, a year–

    BuildingGivesGetsIs paidPaysBetter off

    kWh and € a year. "Better off" compares with no match: selling the extra power to the grid and buying the missing power from it.

    Way 1 · Pay per kWh

    Every month the buildings that got power pay the ones that gave it, at the agreed price. The kWh come from the electricity company's hourly data, so nobody has to count.

    Way 2 · Invest together

    A building pays part of the panels at the start. After that, its share of the power is free. This is the co-investment model of our step 5c.

    How the bill works

    1. Meters. Every building has a smart meter that counts each hour. The roof has its own meter for what it makes.
    2. Split table. The match gives the electricity company the share of every roof for every hour.
    3. Every hour. The company gives each building its share of the roof's kWh and takes it off that building's bill.
    4. Between the buildings. They settle the money themselves, by kWh or by investing. This part is a private agreement.

    MATCHSOL

    Homes are empty at noon. Schools are empty on weekends.

    MATCHSOL matches them, so every solar kilowatt-hour is used in the neighbourhood.

    Today a home roof's extra power at noon is sold to the grid for little money. At the same hour, a school or an office nearby buys power at the full price.

    Weekdays: homes give, schools and offices get

    The sun (yellow) peaks when the school and the office (blue) are open, while the homes (orange) wait for the evening.

    Weekends and summer: schools give, homes get

    The school is closed, so its roof powers the homes, most of all the homes whose roofs are too shaded for panels.

    5 km

    The law. Buildings up to 5 km apart can share one roof's solar power (since 2026), with a different share for every hour (since 2021). Our 9 blocks are less than 1 km across, so any building can match any other.
